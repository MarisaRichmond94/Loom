How do you write conditional text in a scene?

Three approaches for authoring text that varies based on earlier choices

A — Inline Conditional Tags

Write normally. Highlight any phrase and tag it with a condition. Color-coded highlights show which fragments are conditional. Click a highlight to edit the condition or the alternate version.

Scene Editor — Chapter 14
spare_victim = true

The market was quiet. Elara moved through the stalls, eyes low. She kept thinking of his face — alive still, somewhere out there. She bought nothing. She never did anymore.

spare_victim = false

The market was quiet. Elara moved through the stalls, eyes low. The memory she carried was a heavier thing. She bought nothing. She never did anymore.

B — Side-by-Side Variants

The editor splits into columns — one per condition. You write (or paste) both versions in full, seeing them simultaneously. Good for scenes where the variants differ significantly across many lines.

Scene Editor — Chapter 14
Base text (all readers)

The market was quiet that morning. Elara moved through the stalls, eyes low. [fragment_A] She bought nothing. She never did anymore.

spare_victim=true She kept thinking of his face — alive still, somewhere out there.
spare_victim=false The memory she carried was a heavier thing.

C — Base + Overrides

Write the full scene once with placeholder slots. Then define small override snippets per condition that drop into those slots. Efficient when variants are short and the surrounding prose is identical.
